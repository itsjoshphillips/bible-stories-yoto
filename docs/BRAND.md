# Brand: Barley Loaf Bible Stories

**The brand is Barley Loaf: named after the boy whose five barley loaves Jesus
used to feed five thousand (John 6:9). A small thing, offered, that goes a
long way is exactly what a three-minute Bible story for a three-year-old is.**

## Why this name

1. **It comes from inside the product.** Story 42, "Five Loaves and Two Fish",
   is the origin story. The brand explains itself in one sentence to any
   Christian parent, and that sentence is about a child.
2. **It is ownable.** A search of children's Bible audio found no brand using
   it, where the obvious names are crowded: "Little Ark" (books, preschools),
   "Sparrow" (a major Christian record label), "Five Smooth Stones" (many
   ministries), "Lamplight" (a Christian audio drama company). Confidence:
   moderate. This was a web search, not a trade mark search; run the name
   through IP Australia, USPTO and UKIPO before printing anything.
3. **It is warm and concrete.** Bread is a thing a small child knows, it is
   easy to draw at 16 pixels, and it carries no denominational flag.
4. **It scales.** "Barley Loaf" can sit in front of other ages, languages and
   formats without the word "Yoto" or "little" boxing it in.

Runners-up, in order: **Small Sparrow** (Matthew 10:29-31) and
**Stone & Sling** (1 Samuel 17). Both are usable if Barley Loaf fails a trade
mark check.

## The brand in use

| Element | Decision |
|---------|----------|
| Name | Barley Loaf |
| Product line | Barley Loaf Bible Stories |
| Tagline | The Bible's big story, for small ears |
| Promise | Every story true to the passage, told so a three-year-old can follow it |
| Proof points | 60 stories in order; a cast of voices; a parent guide listing every passage; hard stories told plainly and flagged |
| Voice | A calm, well-read parent. Plain words, no hype, no exclamation marks in marketing copy, never cute about hard stories |
| Colours | Deep navy (#16224A), barley gold (#F0C95C), white |
| Mark | A loaf. The cover currently uses an open-licence emoji loaf as a placeholder; commission a simple drawn mark before spending on advertising |

## What the brand must never claim

- That the voices are human. The notices and the guide say plainly that they
  are computer-generated, and every listing must say so too.
- That Yoto endorses it. Use "for Yoto Make Your Own cards" descriptively,
  never Yoto's logo, typeface or card artwork style.
- That the scripts were reviewed by a person, until a person has reviewed them.
