# Go-to-market: Barley Loaf Bible Stories

**Sell on Etsy to capture existing demand and on your own merchant-of-record
store to keep the customers you bring yourself; launch the Christmas Story
pack within ten days; and treat "thousands of buyers" as a 12 to 18 month
target that depends on reaching beyond Yoto owners.** Yoto families who load
their own cards and want Bible content number, on my estimate, only 20,000 to
30,000 households worldwide, so Yoto alone will not deliver thousands.

Written 21 November 2026. Prices and fees are from third-party pages unless
stated and need checking against each platform before launch.

## 1. The case against, first

Three things can stop this reaching thousands, and each has a specific answer.

1. **Computer voices are unpopular with exactly this audience.** Yoto's own
   community forum has a thread titled "Disappointed with the rise in AI
   content", and Yoto now badges AI audio. Some Christian parents will refuse
   it on principle. Answer: disclose it in the first lines of every listing
   (hiding it produces refunds and one-star reviews), lead with what is
   strong (fidelity, order, the parent guide), give away a sampler so the
   audio sells itself, and plan a human-narrated edition if sales justify it.
   The scripts are the asset; the voices are replaceable.
2. **The category is crowded and cheap.** Etsy already has many Bible story
   packs for Yoto at US$5 to US$12 for 8 to 20 stories. Answer: do not
   compete on price per pack. Compete on being the complete, ordered set with
   a passage-by-passage parent guide, and use small seasonal packs as the
   cheap way in.
3. **There is no legal moat.** Text and audio made by AI get little or no
   copyright protection, so a copyist is hard to stop. Answer: the moat is
   brand, reviews, the email list and release cadence, not the files.

Launch is also gated on two things only you can do: listen to all 158 minutes,
and have a person check the scripts against the passages. The guide claims
both.

## 2. Platform: Etsy plus your own store

| Platform | Role | Fee on a US$19 sale | Verdict |
|----------|------|---------------------|---------|
| Etsy | Buyers already search "Bible stories for Yoto" here | About US$2.25 (listing, 6.5% transaction, payment processing), plus 12 to 15% when Etsy's off-site ads make the sale. From memory; verify | Use for discovery |
| Lemon Squeezy | Your own checkout; merchant of record, so it files US sales tax, UK and EU VAT and Australian GST for you | 5% + US$0.50, about US$1.45 (sources disagree on whether card fees are extra) | Use as home base |
| Gumroad | Same role as Lemon Squeezy | 10% + US$0.50, about US$2.40 | Fallback if Lemon Squeezy onboarding is slow |
| Payhip | Cheapest per sale | 5% on the free plan plus card fees | Rejected: not a merchant of record outside UK and EU VAT, so US tax falls on you |
| Shopify | Full shop | Monthly fee, tax on you | Rejected until revenue justifies it |
| Yoto Creators programme | Yoto prints your cards and sells them in its store for a royalty | Royalty | Register interest now; do not plan around it |

Why two, not one:

- **Etsy is where the demand is, but you rent it.** Etsy owns the customer,
  takes the largest cut, and can delist. It also limits digital listings to
  five files of 20 MB each, so the 59 to 146 MB packs must be delivered as a
  small PDF containing a download link, which is the common workaround.
- **Your own store is where the business is.** Traffic you generate (email,
  bloggers, churches) should land where the fee is lowest, you get the email
  address, and you can run affiliates and discount codes.
- **Yoto's own programme is the prize but not a plan.** New creator
  registrations are reported paused, Yoto expects a following on its free
  Open Mic section first, Open Mic review is itself on hold, and I could not
  find its position on computer-generated voices. A competing title, "Big
  Bible Stories for Little Hearts", is already in Yoto's creator store.

Etsy rules that apply (reported, October 2026 update): AI-made digital
downloads are allowed if the seller has a clear creative role and discloses
AI use in the description; audio listings may use representative images.

## 4. Who buys, and how many there are

My own estimate, built bottom-up (confidence: low; only the first figure is
sourced):

| Step | Assumption | Households |
|------|------------|------------|
| Families with a Yoto | "Over 1 million" reported | 1,200,000 |
| With a child aged 3 to 5 now | 40% | 480,000 |
| Who want Bible content for that child | 15% (US-weighted) | 72,000 |
| Who load their own cards | 35% | 25,000 |

Thousands of buyers out of roughly 25,000 would need 10 to 20% of the entire
serviceable Yoto market. That is not credible in 18 months. The same MP3s,
however, serve three larger groups, and the plan depends on them:

1. **Toniebox owners** using Creative-Tonies. Tonies has sold several times
   more players than Yoto. Check capacity first: I believe a Creative-Tonie
   holds 90 minutes, which fits Volume 2 and the seasonal packs but not
   Volume 1 (95 minutes) on one figure.
2. **Christian parents with no audio player at all**, playing MP3s on a phone,
   in the car or at bedtime. This is by far the largest group and the reason
   listings should say "or any MP3 player".
3. **Churches and Christian nurseries**, through the group licence.

A workable target mix for 3,000 buyers in 18 months: about 1,200 from Etsy
search, 1,300 through your own store from email, bloggers and the podcast,
and 500 seasonal-pack buyers who never trade up. At a blended US$14 that is
roughly US$42,000 gross and US$36,000 after fees. This is a worthwhile side
product, not a venture-scale business.

## 5. Channels, in order of expected return

1. **Etsy search.** Ten or so listings, not three: each pack, plus variants
   titled for Yoto, for Toniebox and for "MP3 download". Etsy rewards
   listing count, reviews and recency.
2. **Free sampler into an email list.** Five full stories for an email
   address, followed by four emails over two weeks. This is the only asset
   you fully own and it is what makes Easter cheaper to launch than
   Christmas.
3. **Christian parenting and homeschool writers who already cover Yoto.**
   Homeschooling families are heavily over-represented among Yoto owners.
   Offer a free complete set, a reader discount code and a 30 to 40%
   affiliate commission through the store. Twenty targeted approaches beat
   two hundred cold ones.
4. **Yoto and Toniebox parent groups on Facebook.** These are large and
   allergic to selling. Share the free sampler where rules allow, answer
   questions, and let the sampler do the work.
5. **Churches.** Email children's ministry leaders the sampler and the group
   licence before Advent and before Lent. One church newsletter reaches more
   of the right parents than any advertisement.
6. **A free weekly podcast.** Release one story a week on Apple and Spotify
   with a ten-second pointer to the pack. The audio already exists, so the
   cost is nil; sixty weeks of episodes sell the convenience of having all
   sixty at once, offline, on a card.
7. **Pinterest.** Slow, free and long-lived for Etsy digital products.
8. **Paid advertising.** Etsy ads at a few dollars a day from launch. Meta
   advertising only once the store converts sampler downloads to sales at a
   known rate.

## 7. What to measure

| Measure | Why | Act if |
|---------|-----|--------|
| Sampler download to purchase rate | Tells you whether the audio overcomes the AI objection | Below 3% after 300 downloads: fix the audio or the offer before spending on traffic |
| Etsy listing conversion | Tells you whether the listing and price work | Below 1.5%: rework images, title and price |
| Refund rate and review text | Early warning on voices, theology or delivery problems | Any pattern in complaints |
| Share buying Complete rather than a volume | Tests the price ladder | Below half: widen the gap |
| Email list size | The asset that makes each launch cheaper | Flat for a month |
