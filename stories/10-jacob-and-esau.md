# Fidelity notes
# - Not softened: Jacob's words to his father are called a lie, and Esau's plan
#   to kill him (27:41) is stated.
# - Isaac is an old, blind man here and a boy in story 9, so this script uses
#   the speaker OLD_ISAAC with an older voice.
# - Speech is close paraphrase of 25:30-32, 27:1-4, 27:18-24, 27:33-35 and
#   28:13-16. Rebekah's part (27:5-17) is narrated to keep the cast to five
#   voices; her words to Jacob are not quoted.
# - One explanatory sentence defines the birthright.
# - Interpretive note: the closing sentence observes that God gave the promise
#   to a man who had lied and cheated. That is the plain sequence of the text;
#   it draws no further lesson.
# - Omitted: Isaac's prayer for Rebekah (25:21); the parents' favourites
#   (25:28); "the Lord your God gave me success" (27:20); the kiss and the
#   smell of the clothes; the words of the blessing; Esau's lesser blessing;
#   Laban; the stone pillar, the name Bethel and Jacob's vow (28:18-22).

NARRATOR: [warm, bright, lively storyteller speaking to small children] This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Jacob and Esau.

NARRATOR: [lively] Isaac grew up, and he married Rebekah. And God gave them twin boys. The first to be born was Esau. He was red, and hairy all over. The second was Jacob, born holding his brother's heel. Before they were born, God had told Rebekah a surprising thing. The older one would serve the younger.

NARRATOR: [setting the scene] Esau grew up to be a hunter. Jacob was a quiet man, who stayed among the tents. Because Esau was born first, the birthright was his: the biggest share of everything his father had. One day, Esau came in from the fields, very hungry. And Jacob was cooking red stew.

ESAU: [gruff, impatient] Quick, give me some of that red stew! I am worn out!

JACOB: First, sell me your birthright.

ESAU: I am about to die of hunger! What good is a birthright to me?

NARRATOR: [amused, rueful] So Esau sold his birthright to Jacob, for a bowl of stew. That is how little he cared about it.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [quietly] When Isaac was very old, and could no longer see, he called for Esau.

OLD_ISAAC: [old, frail] My son, go hunting, and cook me the food I love. Then I will give you my blessing, before I die.

NARRATOR: [hushed, secretive] Rebekah was listening. While Esau was out hunting, she cooked a meal herself. She dressed Jacob in Esau's clothes, and covered his smooth hands and neck with hairy goatskin. And she sent Jacob in to his father, with the food.

JACOB: My father.

OLD_ISAAC: Yes? Which of my sons are you?

JACOB: I am Esau, your firstborn. Sit up and eat, and then give me your blessing.

OLD_ISAAC: Come close, and let me touch you, my son.

OLD_ISAAC: [puzzled, feeling his hands] The voice is Jacob's voice. But the hands are the hands of Esau. Are you really my son Esau?

JACOB: I am.

NARRATOR: [serious] It was a lie. But Isaac believed it, and he gave Jacob the blessing. Jacob had only just left, when Esau came in. And Isaac began to tremble all over.

OLD_ISAAC: [shaken] Then who was it that I blessed? Your brother came and tricked me. He has taken your blessing.

ESAU: [a loud, bitter cry] Bless me too, my father!

NARRATOR: [serious] Esau hated Jacob for what he had done, and he planned to kill him. So Jacob had to run away from home.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [hushed, with wonder] When the sun had set, Jacob lay down to sleep on the ground, with a stone for a pillow. And he had a dream. He saw a stairway standing on the earth, with its top reaching up to heaven. The angels of God were going up and down on it. And above it stood the Lord.

GOD: I am the Lord, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac. The land you are lying on, I will give to you and to your children. Through you and your family, every family on earth will be blessed. I am with you. I will watch over you wherever you go. I will not leave you.

NARRATOR: [softly] Jacob woke up.

JACOB: [awed] Surely the Lord is in this place, and I did not know it!

NARRATOR: [warm, amazed] Jacob had lied, and he had cheated. Yet God came to him, and gave him the very promise he had given to Abraham. God keeps his promises.

NARRATOR: [warmly] You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Genesis, chapters twenty-five, twenty-seven and twenty-eight.
