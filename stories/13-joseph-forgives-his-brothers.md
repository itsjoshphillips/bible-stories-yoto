# Fidelity notes
# - Four chapters are heavily compressed. The tests of chapters 42 to 44 (the
#   charge of spying, Simeon held, the money in the sacks, the silver cup) are
#   summarised in two sentences; "tested" is the text's own word (42:15-16).
#   Judah's offer to take Benjamin's place is 44:33.
# - Joseph's speeches are close paraphrase of 45:3-13 and 50:19-21. BROTHER
#   speaks the brothers' shared lines in 50:15 and 50:18.
# - Jacob's words (45:28) are reported by the narrator, as in story 11.
# - "And so the whole family came to live in Egypt" summarises Genesis 46-47.
# - "His brothers meant it for evil, but God meant it for good" is 50:20, the
#   text's own summary, not an added moral.
# - Omitted: the two journeys; the feast; the years of famine remaining
#   (45:6); Goshen by name; Pharaoh's welcome; the message the brothers send
#   in their father's name (50:16-17).

NARRATOR: This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Joseph Forgives His Brothers.

NARRATOR: The seven good years ended, and the hungry years began, just as God had said. Nothing would grow. But in Egypt there was food, because Joseph had stored up grain. People came from every land to buy it.

NARRATOR: Far away in Canaan, Joseph's father Jacob needed food too. So he sent ten of his sons to Egypt, to buy grain. Only Benjamin, the youngest, stayed at home.

NARRATOR: The brothers came before the ruler of Egypt. They bowed down low, with their faces to the ground. They did not know who he was. But Joseph knew them at once. And he remembered his dreams from long ago.

NARRATOR: Joseph did not tell them who he was. Not yet. First, he tested them. And when the test came, his brother Judah offered to become a slave himself, so that young Benjamin could go free.

NARRATOR: Then Joseph could hold it in no longer. He sent all his servants out of the room. And he wept so loudly that the whole house could hear.

JOSEPH: [weeping] I am Joseph! Is my father still alive?

NARRATOR: His brothers could not say a word. They were terrified.

JOSEPH: [gently] Come close to me. I am your brother Joseph, the one you sold into Egypt. But do not be upset, and do not be angry with yourselves. God sent me here ahead of you, to save your lives. It was not you who sent me here. It was God.

NARRATOR: Then Joseph threw his arms around Benjamin, and wept. He kissed every one of his brothers. And after that, they talked together.

JOSEPH: [glad] Now hurry! Go and fetch my father. Bring your children, and your flocks, and come and live near me. I will take care of you.

NARRATOR: The brothers hurried home, and told their father, "Joseph is still alive! He is ruler over all Egypt!" At first, Jacob could not believe it. But then he saw the wagons Joseph had sent to carry him. "It is enough," he said. "My son Joseph is alive. I will go and see him before I die." And so the whole family came to live in Egypt.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: Many years later, Jacob died. And the brothers became afraid again.

BROTHER: [worried] What if Joseph still hates us? What if he pays us back for all the evil we did to him?

NARRATOR: So they came and bowed down in front of him.

BROTHER: We are your slaves.

JOSEPH: [kindly] Do not be afraid. Am I in the place of God? You meant to do me evil. But God meant it for good, to keep many people alive. So do not be afraid. I will take care of you, and your little ones.

NARRATOR: And Joseph comforted them, and spoke kindly to them.

NARRATOR: His brothers meant it for evil. But God meant it for good.

NARRATOR: You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Genesis, chapters forty-two to fifty.
