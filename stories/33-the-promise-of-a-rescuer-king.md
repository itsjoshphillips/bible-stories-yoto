# Fidelity notes
# - A bridge story joining the Old Testament to the New. It summarises rather
#   than retells: the reasons for the exile come from 2 Kings 17:13-15 and
#   2 Chronicles 36:15-16; the fall of Jerusalem from 2 Kings 25:1-11.
# - Chronology kept honest: Isaiah and Micah spoke before the exile, so the
#   script says God gave these promises "long before".
# - Isaiah's and Micah's lines are close paraphrase of Isaiah 9:2, 6-7 and
#   Micah 5:2. All four names of Isaiah 9:6 are kept.
# - "They sat down and wept" by the rivers of Babylon: Psalm 137:1.
# - Not softened: the city broken, the temple burned, the people taken away,
#   and the reason for it.
# - Interpretive gloss, flagged for parental review: the closing lines point
#   forward to Jesus as the promised King (Matthew 2:5-6; Luke 1:32-33).
# - Omitted: the siege and famine, King Zedekiah's fate (25:3-7), the return
#   from exile (Ezra and Nehemiah).

NARRATOR: This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: The Promise of a Rescuer King.

NARRATOR: God loved his people, Israel. He had rescued them from Egypt. He had given them a good land, and good kings like David. And he had given them his good rules.

NARRATOR: But God's people kept turning away from him. They bowed down to pretend gods. They did what was wrong, again and again. God sent messengers, called prophets, to warn them. He sent them many, many times. But the people would not listen.

NARRATOR: So at last, the thing God had warned them about came true.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: The king of Babylon came with a great army. His soldiers broke down the walls of Jerusalem. They burned God's beautiful temple. And they took God's people far away, to the land of Babylon.

NARRATOR: There, beside the rivers of Babylon, God's people sat down and wept. They had no home. They had no temple. They had no king. [quietly] It was a very dark time.

[PAUSE 1.5]

NARRATOR: But God had not forgotten his people. And God had not forgotten his promises. Long before, he had told his prophets wonderful things, and they had written them down. This is what the prophet Isaiah said.

ISAIAH: [warm, with wonder] The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light. For a child is born to us. A son is given to us. He will be our ruler. And he will be called: Wonderful Counsellor. Mighty God. Everlasting Father. Prince of Peace.

NARRATOR: A child, who would be a king!

ISAIAH: He will sit on the throne of King David. And his kingdom, and his peace, will never, ever end.

NARRATOR: And God even told his people where the King would come from. This is what the prophet Micah said.

MICAH: Bethlehem, you are only a little town. But out of you will come a ruler for my people Israel. He comes from long ago, from the very beginning.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: So God's people waited. They waited for the great light. They waited for the child. They waited for the King who would rescue them. They waited for hundreds of years.

NARRATOR: And then, one day, in the little town of Bethlehem. [with quiet delight] But that is the next story.

NARRATOR: God always keeps his promises.

NARRATOR: You can find these promises in the Bible, in the book of Isaiah, chapter nine.
