# Fidelity notes
# - Not softened: the death of the firstborn (12:29-30) is stated plainly,
#   once, including Pharaoh's own son and the crying in every house.
# - All ten plagues are named, in order. "Sickness on the animals" is the
#   death of Egypt's livestock (9:1-7); "sores" are the boils (9:8-12).
# - The refrain "Pharaoh's heart was hard" keeps the text's own phrase. The
#   text also says the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart (9:12, 10:20); that is
#   not explored here.
# - Aaron goes with Moses in the text and speaks for him; here Moses speaks.
# - "Firstborn" is given as "the oldest son", the plain sense for people;
#   the firstborn of the animals (12:29) is omitted.
# - Omitted for length: the harder brick-making (ch. 5), the magicians,
#   Pharaoh's several false promises, the unleavened bread and bitter herbs,
#   the Egyptians' gifts of silver and gold.

NARRATOR: This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: The Plagues and the Passover.

NARRATOR: Moses and his brother Aaron went to Egypt. They stood before Pharaoh, the king, and Moses gave him God's message.

MOSES: This is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says. Let my people go!

PHARAOH: [scornful] Who is the Lord, that I should obey him? I do not know the Lord. And I will not let Israel go.

NARRATOR: So God sent great troubles on Egypt. They are called plagues.

NARRATOR: First, the river turned to blood. Then came frogs. Frogs in the houses, frogs in the beds, frogs in the kitchen bowls! But Pharaoh's heart was hard. He would not let the people go.

NARRATOR: Then came gnats, like dust. Then flies, in great buzzing swarms. Then sickness on the animals. Then sores on people's skin. But Pharaoh's heart was hard. He would not let the people go.

NARRATOR: Then came hail, crashing down from the sky. Then locusts, which ate up every green thing. Then darkness. Thick, thick darkness, for three whole days. But Pharaoh's heart was hard. He would not let the people go.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: Then God told Moses that one last plague was coming. It would be the most terrible of all. And God told his people how to be ready.

GOD: Every family must take a lamb, a perfect one. Kill the lamb in the evening, and paint its blood around the door of your house. Tonight I will pass through Egypt, and every oldest son will die. But when I see the blood, I will pass over you. No harm will come to you.

NARRATOR: God's people did just as he said. They painted the blood around their doors. They roasted the lamb, and they ate it with their sandals on, ready to go.

[PAUSE 1.0]

NARRATOR: [quietly] At midnight, it happened. In every house in Egypt, the oldest son died. Even Pharaoh's own son. There was loud crying all over Egypt.

NARRATOR: But in the houses with the lamb's blood around the door, nobody died. God passed over them, just as he had promised.

NARRATOR: That same night, Pharaoh sent for Moses.

PHARAOH: [broken] Get up! Go! Leave my people, you and all of Israel. Go and worship the Lord, as you said.

NARRATOR: So God's people walked out of Egypt. Thousands and thousands of them: fathers and mothers, children and babies, and all their sheep and cows. They were not slaves any more.

NARRATOR: God told them to remember that night, every single year, with a special meal. It is called the Passover. For on that night, the Lord rescued his people.

NARRATOR: You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Exodus, chapter twelve.
