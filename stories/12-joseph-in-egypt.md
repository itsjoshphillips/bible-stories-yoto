# Fidelity notes
# - Potiphar's wife: the script says she "tried to make Joseph do something
#   very wrong", that he refused in the words of 39:9, and that she lied about
#   him. The nature of the wrong (39:7, 12) is left for an older age.
# - The baker's death (40:19-22) is stated plainly, without the detail.
# - Speech is close paraphrase of 39:9, 40:8, 40:14, 41:9-12, 41:15-16,
#   41:25-36 and 41:38-40.
# - Omitted for length: Potiphar's rank, his anger, Pharaoh's troubled spirit.
# - This Pharaoh is a different king from the Pharaoh of the Exodus stories,
#   so he has his own speaker name, PHARAOH_OF_JOSEPH, and his own voice.
# - Omitted: the details of the cupbearer's and baker's dreams; Pharaoh's
#   second dream of the ears of grain (41:5-7); Joseph's new name, wife and
#   sons (41:45-52); his age (thirty).

NARRATOR: This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Joseph in Egypt.

NARRATOR: Joseph's brothers had sold him, and now he was a slave in Egypt. He worked in the house of a man named Potiphar. But the Lord was with Joseph. Everything Joseph did went well. So Potiphar put him in charge of his whole house.

NARRATOR: Then Potiphar's wife tried to make Joseph do something very wrong. Day after day she asked him. And day after day, Joseph said no.

JOSEPH: How could I do such a wicked thing, and sin against God?

NARRATOR: So she told a lie about Joseph. Potiphar believed her, and he had Joseph thrown into prison.

NARRATOR: But even in prison, the Lord was with Joseph, and was kind to him. The keeper of the prison put Joseph in charge of all the other prisoners.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: Two of those prisoners had worked for Pharaoh, the king of Egypt. One was his cupbearer, who served his drinks. The other was his baker. One night, each of them had a dream. In the morning, their faces were sad.

CUPBEARER: We have had dreams, and there is no one to tell us what they mean.

JOSEPH: Doesn't the meaning of dreams belong to God? Tell me your dreams.

NARRATOR: So they told him. And Joseph told them what the dreams meant. In three days, the cupbearer would get his job back. But in three days, the baker would be put to death.

JOSEPH: [earnest] When things go well for you, please remember me. Tell Pharaoh about me, and get me out of this prison.

NARRATOR: It all happened as Joseph had said. But the cupbearer forgot all about him.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: Two whole years went by. Then Pharaoh himself had a dream. He saw seven fat cows come up out of the river. Then seven thin, ugly cows came up, and ate the fat cows! Pharaoh called for all his wise men. But not one of them could tell him what it meant.

CUPBEARER: [ashamed] Today I remember what I did wrong! There is a young man in the prison, who can tell what dreams mean.

NARRATOR: So Pharaoh sent for Joseph.

PHARAOH_OF_JOSEPH: I have had a dream, and no one can tell me what it means. I have heard that you can.

JOSEPH: I cannot do it. But God will give Pharaoh the answer.

NARRATOR: So Pharaoh told him the dream.

JOSEPH: God is showing Pharaoh what he is about to do. Seven years are coming with plenty of food. After them will come seven years of terrible hunger. So choose a wise man. Let him store up food in the good years, ready for the bad years.

PHARAOH_OF_JOSEPH: Can we find anyone like this man, who has the Spirit of God in him? Joseph, God has shown you all this, so there is no one as wise as you. I put you in charge of all the land of Egypt. Only I, the king, will be greater than you.

NARRATOR: Pharaoh put his own ring on Joseph's finger, and a gold chain around his neck. And Joseph stored up grain, in great heaps, like the sand of the sea.

NARRATOR: In the house, in the prison, and in the palace, the Lord was with Joseph.

NARRATOR: You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Genesis, chapters thirty-nine to forty-one.
