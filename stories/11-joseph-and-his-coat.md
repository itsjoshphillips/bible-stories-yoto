# Fidelity notes
# - Not softened: the brothers' hatred, their plan to kill Joseph, the pit,
#   the sale, the blood on the coat and the lie to their father are all told.
# - BROTHER speaks the lines the text gives to the brothers together (37:8,
#   37:19-20) and to Judah (37:26-27). BROTHER_2 is Reuben (37:21-22).
# - Jacob's words (37:32-33) are reported by the narrator, so that Jacob and
#   Joseph do not need the same voice.
# - "Traders" covers the Ishmaelites and Midianites of 37:25-28.
# - The closing line "the Lord was with Joseph" is taken from Genesis 39:2, the
#   next verse of Joseph's story, so the track does not end in the pit. "God's
#   plan for him was not finished" anticipates 50:20 and is a light gloss.
# - Omitted: Joseph's bad report (37:2); the man at Shechem; Reuben's return to
#   the empty pit (37:29-30); Potiphar's name (it opens story 12).

NARRATOR: [warm, bright, lively storyteller speaking to small children] This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Joseph and His Coat.

NARRATOR: [bright] Jacob had twelve sons. And of all his sons, he loved Joseph the most. He made Joseph a special coat, a beautiful coat of many colours. Joseph's brothers saw that their father loved him more than them. So they hated him. They could not say one kind word to him.

NARRATOR: [lively] When Joseph was seventeen years old, he had a dream. And he told it to his brothers.

JOSEPH: [eager] Listen to my dream! We were tying up bundles of grain in the field. My bundle stood up tall. And your bundles gathered round, and bowed down to mine!

BROTHER: [angry] So you think you are going to be king over us?

NARRATOR: Then Joseph had another dream.

JOSEPH: This time the sun, and the moon, and eleven stars were all bowing down to me!

NARRATOR: [serious] Even his father told him off for that. And his brothers hated him more than ever.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [low, tense] One day, the brothers were far from home, looking after the sheep. Jacob sent Joseph to see how they were. The brothers saw him coming, a long way off.

BROTHER: [scornful] Here comes the dreamer! Come on. Let us kill him, and throw him into a pit. We will say a wild animal ate him. Then we will see what becomes of his dreams!

BROTHER_2: No! Do not kill him. Throw him into this pit, but do not hurt him.

NARRATOR: [hushed] That was Reuben, the eldest. He meant to come back later, and rescue Joseph.

NARRATOR: [quick, dramatic] So when Joseph arrived, his brothers grabbed him. They tore off his beautiful coat. And they threw him down into a deep, dry pit. Then they sat down to eat.

NARRATOR: [brightening] Just then, some traders came by with their camels, on their way to the land of Egypt.

BROTHER: What good will it do us to kill our brother? Come on. Let us sell him to these traders instead.

NARRATOR: [quietly, sad] And so they did. They pulled Joseph up out of the pit, and sold him for twenty pieces of silver. And the traders took Joseph away to Egypt.

[PAUSE 1.0]

NARRATOR: [quietly, serious] Then the brothers killed a goat, and dipped Joseph's coat in its blood. They carried the coat home to their father. "We found this," they said. "Is it your son's coat?"

NARRATOR: [softly, sad] Jacob knew it at once. He thought a wild animal had torn Joseph to pieces. He cried for his son for many, many days, and no one could comfort him.

NARRATOR: [gently, hopeful] Far away in Egypt, Joseph was sold as a slave. He had lost his coat, his home, and his father. But the Lord was with Joseph. And God's plan for him was not finished.

NARRATOR: [warmly] You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Genesis, chapter thirty-seven.
