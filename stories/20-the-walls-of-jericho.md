# Fidelity notes
# - Judgement call, flagged for parental review: Joshua 6:21 says the city
#   and everyone in it were destroyed. The script says so in one plain
#   sentence. The plan does not mark this story as weighty; consider marking
#   it so.
# - Rahab's occupation (2:1) is omitted. Her words are close paraphrase of
#   2:9-13; her rescue is 6:22-25.
# - God's words to Joshua are from 1:9 and 6:2-5; Joshua's shout is 6:16.
# - Omitted for length: the crossing of the Jordan (ch. 3-4, outside the
#   passage), the king's search party, the ark carried in the march, the
#   commander of the Lord's army (5:13-15), the ban on plunder.

NARRATOR: [warm, bright, lively storyteller speaking to small children] This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: The Walls of Jericho.

NARRATOR: [setting the scene] Moses had grown very old, and he had died. Now God chose a new leader for his people. His name was Joshua. It was time, at last, to go into the land God had promised them.

GOD: [calm, warm] Be strong, and be brave. Do not be afraid, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.

NARRATOR: [ominous] But in that land stood a city called Jericho. It had great, high walls all the way round, and strong gates, shut tight.

NARRATOR: [hushed] Joshua sent two men to look at Jericho in secret. They stayed at the house of a woman named Rahab. The king of Jericho heard about it, and he sent soldiers to catch them. But Rahab hid the two men up on her roof.

RAHAB: [hushed, earnest] I know that the Lord has given you this land. Your God is God in heaven above, and on the earth below. Please, be kind to me and my family, as I have been kind to you.

NARRATOR: [reassuring] The two men promised. They told her to tie a red cord in her window, and her family would be safe. Then Rahab let them down by a rope, for her house was built into the city wall.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [curious] Then God told Joshua what to do. And it was a very strange way to fight.

GOD: See, I have given Jericho into your hands. March around the city, once a day, for six days. On the seventh day, march around it seven times. Then the priests shall blow their trumpets, and all the people shall shout. And the wall will fall down flat.

NARRATOR: [steady, rhythmic] So that is what they did. On the first day, they marched around Jericho, once. The priests blew their trumpets. But nobody said a word.

NARRATOR: [building slowly] On the second day, they marched around, once. And the third day. And the fourth day. And the fifth. And the sixth.

NARRATOR: [building excitement] On the seventh day, they got up very early. They marched around the city one time. Two times. Three, four, five, six times. Seven times! Then the priests blew a long, loud blast on the trumpets.

[SFX: a long blast on a ram's horn trumpet | 3]

JOSHUA: [shouting] Shout! For the Lord has given you the city!

NARRATOR: [excited, amazed] And all the people gave a great shout. And the walls of Jericho fell down flat!

[PAUSE 0.8]

NARRATOR: [quietly, serious] God's people went straight in, and they took the city. Jericho was destroyed, and so were the people in it.

NARRATOR: [brightening] But not Rahab. The two men went to her house and brought her out, with her father, and her mother, and all her family. They were safe. And Rahab lived among God's people from that day on.

NARRATOR: [with wonder] No sword knocked down those walls. The Lord did it, just as he had said.

NARRATOR: [warmly] You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Joshua, chapter six.
