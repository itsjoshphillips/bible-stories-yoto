# Fidelity notes
# - Not softened: Pharaoh's order to throw every baby boy into the river
#   (1:22) is stated plainly, once.
# - The sister is unnamed in Exodus 2. The speaker label MIRIAM follows
#   Exodus 15:20 and Numbers 26:59; the name is never spoken aloud.
# - "The people of Israel" is used throughout where the text has "Hebrews".
# - Interpretive gloss, flagged for parental review: "God kept this baby
#   safe." God is not named in 2:1-10; the gloss rests on Acts 7:20-21 and
#   Hebrews 11:23. "A great work for Moses to do" looks ahead to Exodus 3:10.
# - Omitted for length: the midwives Shiphrah and Puah (1:15-21), the names
#   of the store cities, the tribe of Levi.

NARRATOR: This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Baby Moses in the Basket.

NARRATOR: Joseph's family had come to live in Egypt. Years and years went by. The family grew, and grew, and grew, until the land was full of them. They were called the people of Israel.

NARRATOR: Then a new king ruled Egypt. He was called Pharaoh, and he knew nothing about Joseph. Pharaoh looked at God's people, and he did not like what he saw.

PHARAOH: [cold, proud] There are too many of them. They are too strong for us.

NARRATOR: So Pharaoh made God's people his slaves. He made them work hard, making bricks, and building his cities. It was a bitter life. But the harder they worked, the more God's people grew.

NARRATOR: Then Pharaoh gave a terrible order.

PHARAOH: Every baby boy born to the people of Israel must be thrown into the river. Only the girls may live.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: Now, one mother in Israel had a baby boy. He was a beautiful baby. She hid him away, so that nobody would find him. For three months she kept him hidden. But then she could not hide him any longer.

NARRATOR: So she took a basket made of reeds, and she covered it with sticky tar, to keep the water out. She laid her baby inside. And she put the basket among the tall reeds, at the edge of the great river. The baby's big sister stood a little way off, to see what would happen.

NARRATOR: Then somebody came down to the river to wash. It was the princess: Pharaoh's own daughter! She saw the basket in the reeds, and she sent her servant girl to fetch it. The princess opened the basket. And there was the baby, crying. She felt sorry for him.

PRINCESS: [tenderly] This is one of the babies of Israel.

NARRATOR: Then the baby's sister spoke to the princess.

MIRIAM: Shall I go and find a woman of Israel, to nurse the baby for you?

PRINCESS: Yes. Go.

NARRATOR: And who do you think the girl fetched? The baby's own mother!

PRINCESS: Take this baby, and nurse him for me. I will pay you.

NARRATOR: So the mother took her own little boy, and she cared for him. And when he was older, she brought him to the princess, and he became the princess's son. The princess gave him his name.

PRINCESS: I will call him Moses, because I drew him out of the water.

NARRATOR: Pharaoh wanted every baby boy in Israel dead. But God kept this baby safe, right inside Pharaoh's own house. And God had a great work for Moses to do.

NARRATOR: You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Exodus, chapters one and two.
