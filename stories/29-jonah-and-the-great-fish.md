# Fidelity notes
# - Speech is close paraphrase of Jonah 1:2, 6, 9, 12; 2:9; 3:4; 4:2, 10-11.
# - "A great fish" as in the text (1:17), not a whale.
# - The book ends on God's unanswered question (4:11); so does this script.
# - Not softened: Nineveh's wickedness, the warning of destruction, and
#   Jonah's anger at God's mercy are all kept.
# - Compressed: the casting of lots and the sailors' questions (1:7-11), the
#   sailors' prayer and sacrifice (1:14-16), Jonah's psalm (chapter 2) to one
#   line, the king's decree (3:6-9), the worm and the east wind (4:7-8).

NARRATOR: [warm, bright, lively storyteller speaking to small children] This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Jonah and the Great Fish.

NARRATOR: One day, God spoke to a man named Jonah.

GOD: Get up, Jonah. Go to the great city of Nineveh, and speak against it. I have seen how wicked its people are.

NARRATOR: [mischievous, hurried] But Jonah did not go to Nineveh. He ran away. He went down to the sea, and he found a ship that was sailing far, far the other way. He paid his money, and he climbed on board.

[SFX: storm wind at sea, a wooden ship creaking | 4]

NARRATOR: [building excitement] Then God sent a great wind over the sea. The storm was so strong that the ship was about to break apart. The sailors were terrified. They threw their boxes and bags into the sea. And Jonah? He was down inside the ship, fast asleep. The captain shook him.

SAILOR: [urgent] How can you sleep? Get up, and call on your god!

NARRATOR: [hushed] Then the sailors found out that the storm had come because of Jonah.

JONAH: I worship the Lord, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land. Pick me up, and throw me into the sea. Then the sea will grow calm. I know this storm is my fault.

NARRATOR: [tense] The sailors did not want to. They rowed as hard as they could. But the storm only grew worse. So they picked Jonah up, and they threw him into the sea. And the sea grew still.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [slow, wide-eyed] Down, down, down went Jonah. But God sent a great fish, and the fish swallowed Jonah whole. Jonah was inside the fish for three days and three nights. And from inside the fish, Jonah prayed.

JONAH: [quietly] Lord, I called to you, and you heard me. It is the Lord who saves!

NARRATOR: [amused] Then God spoke to the fish. And the fish spat Jonah out, onto dry land.

NARRATOR: God spoke to Jonah a second time.

GOD: Get up. Go to the great city of Nineveh.

NARRATOR: [brightly] This time, Jonah went. He walked into the city, and he called out,

JONAH: [calling out] In forty days, Nineveh will be destroyed!

NARRATOR: [amazed] And the people of Nineveh believed God. All of them, from the greatest to the least, even the king. They were sorry, and they turned away from the wicked things they did. God saw it. And he did not destroy them.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [puzzled] But Jonah was not glad. Jonah was angry.

JONAH: [sulking] I knew it! I knew that you are a kind God, slow to get angry, and full of love. That is why I ran away.

NARRATOR: [gently] Jonah sat down outside the city. God made a leafy plant grow up to shade his head, and Jonah was very happy about the plant. But the next day, the plant died. And Jonah was angry all over again.

GOD: [gently] Jonah. You care about a plant. You did not make it, and you did not grow it. Nineveh has more than a hundred and twenty thousand people, and many animals too. Should I not care about that great city?

NARRATOR: You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Jonah.
