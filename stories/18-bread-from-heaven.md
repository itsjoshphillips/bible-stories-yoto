# Fidelity notes
# - The people's grumbling is kept at full strength (16:3, 17:3), including
#   the accusation that Moses brought them out to die.
# - The worms and the smell in the manna kept overnight (16:20) are kept.
# - "What is it?" is the meaning of the word manna (16:15, 16:31).
# - The closing question "Is the Lord with us, or not?" is the text's own
#   (17:7); the answer given is the plain sense of the two events.
# - Forty years of manna comes from 16:35.
# - Omitted for length: the measure (an omer), the jar of manna kept as a
#   reminder, those who went out on the seventh day, the place names Massah
#   and Meribah, Moses's fear of being stoned (17:4).

NARRATOR: [warm, bright, lively storyteller speaking to small children] This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Bread from Heaven.

NARRATOR: [setting the scene] God's people were walking through the desert. It was hot, and dry, and empty. There were no fields, and no shops, and nothing to eat. And the people began to grumble.

CROWD: [whining] We wish we had died in Egypt! There we had pots of meat, and all the bread we wanted.

CROWD_2: [complaining] You have brought us out here to die of hunger!

NARRATOR: [gently] God heard their grumbling. And he spoke to Moses.

GOD: [calm, warm] I am going to rain down bread from heaven for you. Every day, the people shall go out and gather enough for that day.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [lively] That evening, birds called quail came flying in, and they covered the camp. So the people had meat to eat.

NARRATOR: [curious] And in the morning, when the dew dried up, there was something on the ground. It was small, and white, and thin as frost. The people stared at it.

CROWD: What is it?

MOSES: It is the bread the Lord has given you to eat.

NARRATOR: [delighted] They called it manna. That means, "What is it?" And it tasted like wafers made with honey.

NARRATOR: [cheerful] Everybody gathered some. Some gathered a lot, and some gathered a little. But everybody had just enough.

MOSES: Do not keep any until morning.

NARRATOR: [amused] But some people did not listen. They kept some overnight. And in the morning, it was full of worms, and it smelled horrible!

NARRATOR: [brightening] On the sixth day, God gave them twice as much, and that did not go bad. For the seventh day was a day of rest, and nobody had to gather at all.

NARRATOR: [with wonder] Morning after morning, for forty years, God gave his people bread from heaven.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: [a little weary] The people walked on, and they came to a place with no water. And they grumbled all over again.

CROWD_2: [angry] Give us water to drink! Why did you bring us out of Egypt? To make us die of thirst?

NARRATOR: [quietly] Moses cried out to God.

MOSES: [desperate] What shall I do with these people?

GOD: Take your staff, and walk on ahead. I will stand before you there, on the rock. Strike the rock, and water will come out of it.

NARRATOR: [excited] Moses struck the rock with his staff. And out came water! Enough for everybody to drink.

NARRATOR: [warm] The people had asked, "Is the Lord with us, or not?" He was. He gave them bread when they were hungry, and water when they were thirsty, even though they grumbled.

NARRATOR: [warmly] You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Exodus, chapter sixteen.
