# Fidelity notes
# - Speech is close paraphrase of Esther 4:11, 13-14, 16; 5:3; 7:3-6.
# - Not softened: Haman's plan to kill all the Jews, the death penalty for
#   coming to the king unasked, and Haman's death (7:10), stated once each.
# - Omitted as gore: the gallows, and Haman being hanged on the one he built
#   for Mordecai (5:14; 7:9-10).
# - Interpretive gloss, flagged for parental review: the book of Esther never
#   names God. The line "God had kept his people safe" is the reading the rest
#   of the Bible supports (see Genesis 12:2-3; Psalm 121:4); strike it to keep
#   the book's own silence.
# - The king is Ahasuerus in the Hebrew text, often identified as Xerxes; the
#   script simply says "the king".
# - Compressed: two feasts into one (5:4-8; 7:1-2); the sleepless night and
#   Mordecai's honour (chapter 6) omitted; the second law (8:8-14) to one line.

NARRATOR: This is a story from God's book, the Bible. It is called: Brave Queen Esther.

NARRATOR: Many of God's people, the Jews, were living far from home, in the land of Persia. One of them was a young woman named Esther. She had no mother or father, so her cousin Mordecai had brought her up as his own daughter.

NARRATOR: The king of Persia was looking for a new queen. And out of all the young women in the land, he chose Esther. He put a crown on her head. But Esther told nobody that she was a Jew.

NARRATOR: Now, the king had a helper named Haman. Haman was proud. Everybody had to bow down to him. But Mordecai would not bow. Haman was so angry that he made a terrible plan. He would kill Mordecai, and all the Jews in the whole kingdom. He tricked the king into making it the law.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: When Mordecai heard, he cried out loud in the streets. He sent a message to Esther in the palace. He begged her to go to the king, and ask him to save her people.

ESTHER: [worried] Everybody knows the rule. Nobody may go to the king unless he calls for them. Anyone who does is put to death, unless the king holds out his golden sceptre. And he has not called for me in thirty days.

MORDECAI: Do not think you will be safe in the palace. And who knows? Perhaps you were made queen for such a time as this.

ESTHER: [resolved] Then gather all our people, and go without food for three days, for me. After that, I will go to the king, even though it is against the law. And if I die, I die.

[SCENE]

NARRATOR: On the third day, Esther put on her royal robes. She walked into the king's court, and she stood there, waiting.

[PAUSE 1.0]

NARRATOR: The king saw her. And he held out his golden sceptre.

KING: What is it, Queen Esther? What do you want? I will give it to you, even if it is half my kingdom.

NARRATOR: Esther asked the king and Haman to come to a feast. And there, at the table, she told him.

ESTHER: [pleading] O king, please let me live. And let my people live. For we have been sold, my people and I, to be killed.

KING: [shocked] Who would dare to do such a thing? Where is he?

ESTHER: [clear] Our enemy is this wicked Haman!

NARRATOR: Haman was terrified. And the king was furious. That very day, he had Haman put to death.

NARRATOR: Then the king gave Mordecai Haman's place. And a new law was written, so that the Jews could stand up and fight for their lives. They were saved. And in every town, there was gladness, and joy, and feasting.

NARRATOR: Esther had risked her life for her people. And God had kept his people safe.

NARRATOR: You can find this story in the Bible, in the book of Esther.
